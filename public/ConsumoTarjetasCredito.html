Credit Card Client Segmentation¶

Business Context and Problem¶

A Colombian bank has spending data for more than 47,000 credit cardholders, but has no way to group these clients according to how they use their card. Without this segmentation, promotions are designed generically, without taking advantage of consumption, spending and behavior patterns.

Goal: segment clients using K-Means clustering based on their transaction behavior, to enable more targeted, data-driven promotional strategies.

#Build the clustering input: after testing, using ALL variables together produced an elbow curve with no visible bend
#(too many correlated/redundant features flatten the inertia curve). Reducing to the 2 variables that best differentiate
#spending behavior -- number of transactions and average transaction amount -- and log-transforming them (both are highly
#skewed: most clients have very few transactions, a handful have many) produces a much clearer elbow.
import numpy as np
from sklearn.preprocessing import StandardScaler

cluster_vars = df_cluster[['number_of_transactions', 'avg_transaction_amount']].copy()
cluster_vars['number_of_transactions'] = np.log1p(cluster_vars['number_of_transactions'])
cluster_vars['avg_transaction_amount'] = np.log1p(cluster_vars['avg_transaction_amount'])

scaler = StandardScaler()
array_norm = scaler.fit_transform(cluster_vars)

Elbow Method to determine the optimal number of clusters¶

In [49]:
#Evaluate from 1 to 6 clusters.
from sklearn.cluster import KMeans
inercias = []
for k in range(1, 7):
  kmeans = KMeans(n_clusters=k, random_state=42, n_init=10)
  kmeans.fit(array_norm)
  inercias.append(kmeans.inertia_)

Final Analysis¶

Cluster 0 is the largest group, with 42.7% of clients (20,436 clients). It is defined by low transaction frequency (around 2 transactions) and the lowest average transaction amount ($98,580), making it the bank's "occasional, low-spend" segment. Mastercard is the preferred card (57.6% of spending) for domestic purchases (96.9% of spending stays domestic, the highest share of the three clusters), and "Supermarkets / Express Stores" is the dominant merchant category.

Cluster 1 represents 33.1% of clients (15,824 clients). Like Cluster 0, these clients transact infrequently (around 2 transactions), but their average transaction amount is by far the highest of the three clusters at $846,526 — nearly 9 times higher than Cluster 0. This is the bank's "occasional, high-value" segment: clients who make large purchases when they do use the card. Mastercard is again the leading franchise (52.4%), and this cluster has the highest share of international spending (10.3%) among the three.

Cluster 2 is the smallest group at 24.2% of clients (11,611 clients), but it stands out for transaction frequency: these clients average 14.3 transactions, roughly 6-7 times more often than the other two clusters, with a mid-range average transaction amount ($204,892). This is the bank's "frequent user" segment. Mastercard remains dominant (60.4% of spending), and international spending (10.2%) is close to Cluster 1's level.

Across all three clusters, spending is concentrated in the afternoon and on Saturdays, and "Supermarkets / Express Stores" is consistently the top merchant category — so timing and merchant category do not differentiate the segments. The real distinction between clusters is how often clients transact and how much they spend per transaction: Cluster 0 (frequent-low), Cluster 1 (rare-high-value), and Cluster 2 (frequent-regular) represent three distinct spending behaviors that a one-size-fits-all promotion strategy would miss.

Business Recommendations¶

Because the three clusters differ mainly in transaction frequency and spend per transaction, promotions should be tailored to each behavior rather than applied uniformly:

Cluster 0 (occasional, low-spend, 42.7% of clients): the priority is increasing engagement. A Mastercard-linked cashback or points promotion on "Supermarkets / Express Stores" purchases — their dominant category — could encourage more frequent card usage among a segment that currently transacts very rarely.

Cluster 1 (occasional, high-value, 33.1% of clients): since these clients already spend large amounts per transaction, the opportunity is retention and share-of-wallet growth rather than frequency. A premium benefits program (travel insurance, airport lounge access, or purchase protection tied to Mastercard) would fit their profile and the slightly higher international spending share (10.3%) seen in this group.

Cluster 2 (frequent users, 24.2% of clients): this group already transacts often, so the goal is to deepen loyalty. A tiered rewards program that increases benefits with transaction volume would reinforce their existing behavior and could help migrate clients from Cluster 0 toward this more active profile over time.

As a next step, since merchant category and timing patterns are similar across all clusters, a deeper analysis of what these clients purchase within "Supermarkets / Express Stores" and other frequent categories would help design more specific, product-level promotions.
